**Result**: The charter gave us a clear reference point when deciding who owns a task and how our stand-ups should run. I learned that a charter is only useful if it is revisited as the team and project change. Next time I would review it with the team at the end of each sprint retrospective.

## 2. User Stories and Tasks

### Evidence
Each sprint-day card on Trello contains a **user story** in the description and a **subtasks checklist**, with an owner named on every task. The worked example below is **Sprint 2: Customer Booking & Ordering**.

![Sprint 2 Day 1 card with user story and subtasks](images/user-story-sprint-2-day-1.png)

| Sprint 2 card | User story | Tasks (owner) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Day 1: Customer Booking & Ordering** | As a customer, I want to book or order online, so I don't rely on WhatsApp or calls. | Build service/product listing on the booking page (Ncebakazi); allow the customer to select a service or item, e.g. haircut or kota meal (Rose); display price and estimated duration (Somila) |
| **Day 2: Date & Time Selection** | As a customer, I want to pick a date and time slot, so I can book when it suits me. | Build the calendar/time-slot picker (Ncebakazi); plus supporting tasks for Rose and Somila as listed on the card |
| **Day 3: Booking Confirmation** | As a customer, I want to see a confirmation of my booking, so I know it is secured. | Confirmation tasks as listed on the Trello card (3/3 complete) |
| **Day 4: Payment screen/confirmation** | As a customer, I want a payment screen, so I can confirm what I owe for my booking or order. | Payment screen tasks (checklist to be added on Trello) |

The Sprint 1 cards follow the same format, for example *"As a dev team, we want the environment and design set up, so we can start building smoothly"*:

![Sprint 1 Day 1 card](images/user-story-sprint-1-day-1.png)

### Reflection
**Situation**: Our product backlog items were broad, and the team needed to know exactly what each person had to do each day of a sprint.
**Task**: As Scrum Master I had to turn the backlog into sprint-day cards with user stories and tasks, each with a clear owner.
**Action**: I created the Sprint 1 and Sprint 2 lists on 06-08-2026 and wrote user stories in the "As a ..., I want ..., so ..." format, then broke each into subtasks with one name per task. I took the service listing and the calendar/time-slot picker myself so that I was also contributing to delivery, not only coordinating.
**Result**: Everyone could see their own work and its purpose, which cut down on confusion in stand-ups. Writing from the customer's point of view kept us focused on the real problem of replacing WhatsApp and phone bookings. I learned that a good user story makes a task easier to test, and next time I would add acceptance criteria to every card.

### Reflection
**Situation**: At the start of the project the team had a lot of ideas but no single prioritised list of what the product needed to do.
**Task**: As Scrum Master and the person responsible for backlog management, I had to create and maintain the Product Backlog.
**Action**: I built the Trello board, added my teammates, wrote the backlog cards and grouped them by sprint with colour labels. I asked Rose and Somila to review the list and later reorganised the cards into sprint-day cards with user stories and subtasks.

### Reflection
**Situation**: The assessment required a regular record of what each team member was doing and what was getting in the way.
**Task**: As Scrum Master I had to keep my own logbook up to date and encourage the team to do the same.
**Action**: After each working session I recorded what I had completed, what I planned next and any impediments, and I used the taskboard and walkthrough questions to check whether anyone was working on something that was not on the board.
**Result**: The logbook showed me where our time was going and highlighted blockers such as time management and waiting on the backend. I learned that logging should happen on the day, not later, because details like why a task was reopened are easy to forget. Next time I would write my entry straight after each stand-up.

## Overall reflection as Scrum Master
Being Scrum Master taught me that the role is about enabling the team: keeping the backlog clear, making work visible on the board, facilitating stand-ups and removing blockers, while still contributing as a frontend developer and tester. My biggest lesson was that Agile artefacts (the charter, backlog, sprint lists and logbook) only help when they are kept up to date and agree with each other.
